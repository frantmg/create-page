# Rules: materials (section 2)

## 2. Read the materials

page-content reads the facts, compliance documents, current site facts and voice, and the benchmark. page-designer reads the logo, brand guide and the current site's look. Both follow the priority rule at the end.

- **Logo:** the orchestrator copies it into `<work>/materials/` (from the attachment or the current website) and the brief gives its path, or says `Logo: none`. page-designer always runs `scripts/logo.py` on it: it trims empty space, resizes raster logos to 400px wide at most, writes a data URI (or a cleaned inline SVG), and reports whether the logo is light or dark plus its main color. Embed the result in the header and the footer with `alt="[Practice name] logo"`. A light logo sits only on a dark or brand-color background, never on white. With no logo, use the practice name as a text wordmark in the display font. The logo spot is never empty.
- **Brand guidelines:** pull the primary brand color (the color seed), any secondary or accent color, the fonts, and voice rules. Brand rules win over the defaults in this kit when they conflict, except the reading level and compliance rules.
- **Brand guide = the limits of the design.** When a brand guide is shared, the orchestrator writes `<work>/brand.json` from it before the build, using only what the guide says (never filling a field from taste; write `unspecified` when the guide is silent):

```json
{
  "source": "materials/<brand guide file>",
  "colors": { "primary": "#HEX", "secondary": ["#HEX"], "accent": ["#HEX"], "neutrals": ["#HEX"] },
  "fonts": { "display": "Font name", "body": "Font name" },
  "corners": { "buttons": "square | rounded | pill | unspecified", "media": "square | rounded | unspecified", "radius_px": null },
  "gradients": "allowed | not allowed | unspecified",
  "decorative_elements": "allowed | approval required | not allowed | unspecified",
  "photo_style": "what the guide says about imagery",
  "dont": ["each rule the guide states as a don't, in its own words"],
  "approved": {}
}
```

  With `brand.json` present the page stays inside the guide: its colors (no invented accent color), its fonts as designed, its corner style, and **no decorative style the guide does not allow or mention** (no ornaments, grain, patterns, gradients or uneven shapes). This wins over the art-direction defaults in section 5 (the gradient moment, grain, signature shapes). Where the guide is silent, use the most neutral option (even, plain corners; solid brand color; no decoration) and list each one as an assumption for the user to confirm. Something beyond the guide goes in only when the user explicitly approves it; record it under `approved`. `scripts/qa.py --brand <work>/brand.json` checks the finished page against it.
- **Compliance documents:** list every rule that affects copy, claims, testimonials, images, forms or disclaimers. Follow them strictly. They override everything in this kit, including the reading level if a required disclaimer must be word-for-word.
- **Practice facts:** collect what is real. Anything not provided stays a `[placeholder]`.
- **Content document:** when the user shares their own content (a Word, PDF or text file with the page's words), it is in `<work>/materials/` and the brief says `Content: document`. page-content uses its words as described in `rules/copy.md` 4.3.
- **Current website:** the orchestrator runs `scripts/fetch_site.py` during the intake, which saves `<work>/materials/site/summary.json` (title, phones, emails, address lines, navigation, headings, the colors and fonts the site uses, the logo) and `text.md` (each page's readable text). Read those first; open the site yourself (WebFetch, or the browser if it needs rendering) only for what they do not have. Pull real practice facts (services, providers, locations, hours, insurance, phone), the logo, colors, fonts and voice in use. Treat these facts as provided by the practice, but flag anything that looks out of date. When there is no brand guide, the current site is the brand reference.
- **Benchmark website:** open it and take **structure ideas only**: section order, which kinds of modules it uses, how it leads to the CTA, how the form or quiz flows, how much content it shows. Write 3 to 5 short takeaways and map each one to blocks from the library (3.1). Never take its colors, fonts, imagery, logo or copy.
- **Priority when sources disagree:** compliance documents, then brand guidelines, then the current website, then the art direction in section 5. The benchmark only informs structure and never overrides any of these.

### 3.3 Selection rules

1. **The hero carries Message 1 and the primary CTA.** The H1 states Message 1 in plain words. (In conversion mode the H1 states the offer in the keyword's words instead, and Message 1 goes in the line under it and the first bullet.)
2. **Messages 1, 2, 3 get the next sections, in that order.** Pick the block by the kind of message:
   - Speed or access ("seen this week") → M10 or M08-A
   - Trust or credentials → M10, M18, M12 or M26
   - Treatment options or "no surgery first" → M08-B, M13 or M14
   - How it works or ease → M11-A or M05-B
   - Cost or insurance → M25 or M09
   - Convenience or locations → M19, M32 or M33
   - Results → M09, M23-B (consent only), M26
3. **Repeat the primary CTA** in the hero, at least once mid-page, and in the final section. Every CTA uses the exact same words. A page has **at most 2 distinct actions** (for example: the form and a call button). Repeating the same action in several places is fine; adding a third action is not.
4. **Landing pages have one goal.** No main nav, no blog, no outbound links except legal and phone. Secondary CTA only if the user gave one.
5. **Do not repeat a block type** on one page unless the content truly needs it. One pull quote max.
6. **Choose variants deliberately:** A vs B by what content exists (no good photo means M01-B with a smaller image or M02-B, not M01-A).
7. **Drop a block if its content does not exist.** A stats block with invented numbers is worse than no stats block.

### 3.4 Conversion mode (landing pages and pages built for conversion)

Three priorities rule the content and structure: **simplicity, scanability, and one clear goal.** Simplicity means fewer messages, fewer fields and fewer actions. It never means less color, plainer type or a quieter layout.

**Conversion mode does not touch the visual design.** It decides what goes on the page, in what order, and how leads come in. Everything about how the page looks (color, gradients, type, imagery, layout, motion) comes from section 5, in full, exactly as on any other page. A landing page should look at least as rich and specific to the brand as the home page.

**Conversion recipe** (in this order; bracketed blocks only when the content exists):

1. **LP header:** logo + call button. If Lead flow is "Call first", the call button is the main action in the header and stays reachable (sticky) after the first scroll.
2. **M01-B hero, above the fold on a phone:** H1 = the offer, in the keyword's words (message match: "Orthopedic Surgeon in Layton", not "Expert care for you"). One line saying who provides it (practice name). 3 bullets with the key facts. Primary CTA (+ call as the second action). Trust signals (rating, years, insurance). On a 390 x 844 phone screen, the H1, the who line and the primary CTA must all show without scrolling.
3. **[M22-A doctor video (VSL)]** right after the hero when a video exists: the doctor explains the practice or procedure. Load it on click (poster image first) so the page stays fast.
4. **Message 1, 2, 3 blocks**, bullets first, short. Use the block-by-message rules in 3.3.
5. **Proof:** [M23-B before/after] high on the page for aesthetic and surgical specialties (consent and disclosure required), M26-B or M26-A real reviews, [M25] insurance badges.
6. **M11-A steps:** how to get started, 3 steps.
7. **Conversion zone:** the form that fits Lead flow:
   - *Short form:* only the fields needed to qualify (usually first name, phone, and one choice like location). Right for clients with a call center that calls back fast.
   - *Multi-step form:* step 1 is low effort (name + phone or one easy choice), then 1 to 3 more short screens with qualifying questions, with a progress bar. Each finished step builds momentum. Right when the client needs more detail before a call.
   - *Call first:* big call button block (M28-B with phone as the main action), form as the second action.
   - *Quiz funnel (QZ block):* 3 to 5 tappable questions, one per screen, with a progress bar (large tappable option cards built on radio inputs), ending in a short contact step and a plain result ("You may be a good fit for a visit."). Best for consideration campaigns and social traffic. Never present the result as a diagnosis.
8. **M31 FAQ, below the conversion zone:** answer the main doubts: recovery time, cost and insurance, pain, and who is a good candidate. Answers come from the practice; use `[placeholders]` until they do.
9. **Keyword block, below the fold:** a short section (M07 or M09 style) that uses the target keyword and close variants naturally, for ad relevance. It must still read at grade 5 and must not crowd the top of the page.
10. **M28-B final CTA**, same words as the hero CTA.
11. **LP footer:** medical disclaimer, before/after disclosure if used, legal links.

**Copy rules (on top of section 4):**
- Make the offer clear above the fold: what it is and who offers it.
- Put key facts in bullets, never in dense paragraphs.
- Use low-friction CTA words: "Schedule a conversation", "Book a visit", "See if you're a fit". Avoid formal words like "consultation" on buttons.
- Treat the ad and the page as one piece: the H1 echoes the keyword and ad headline. The keyword phrase may be used as-is even if it raises the grade a little; pass its words to the checker with `--allow`.

**Action rule:** the primary CTA must be easy to find on every screen of the phone view. How it looks (color, shape, motion) comes from section 5.

If the user chose "Show me the plan first", show the plan as a wireframe plus a short list. Run `python3 <kit>/scripts/wireframe.py <work>/content/plan.md`: it stacks the chosen blocks from `blocks/` in plan order (desktop and mobile) into `<work>/wireframe.html`, so the user sees the page before approving it. # Rules: copy at grade 5 (section 4)

## 4. Write the copy at grade 5

Owned by page-content. Every word goes in `content/content.json` (shape in 4.1); the designer renders it as written. The intake sets the content mode: **write** (page-content writes every word), **document** (the client's own content document is the source, 4.3) or **lorem** (placeholder text, 4.2). Every word on the page is written for a 5th-grade reader. Target Flesch-Kincaid grade **5.0**, hard maximum **5.9** for the page and for each block.

**Rules**

- Short sentences. Average 8 to 12 words. Never more than 15.
- Short words. Prefer one and two syllables.
- Talk to the reader: "you" and "we".
- Active voice. One idea per sentence.
- Headlines: 8 words or fewer, say the benefit. ("Walk without knee pain again.")
- Buttons: start with a verb, 2 to 4 words. ("Book a visit", "Call now", "Check my insurance".)
- Bullets: 2 to 6 words each.
- Medical names are allowed when they are the real name of a condition or treatment. Say the name once, then explain it in plain words right after. ("Sciatica is pain that runs from your low back down your leg.") Pass these words to the checker with `--allow`.
- No em dashes. Use periods, commas, colons or parentheses.
- Avoid generated-page tells: no single word in a headline set in a different color or italic, no uppercase except selectively on short labels, no labels above every heading, no meta strings joined with middle dots, no "→" added to button or link text.
- Never trade accuracy for simplicity. If a plain word changes the medical meaning, keep the right word and explain it.

**Swaps**

| Instead of | Write |
|---|---|
| appointment (when it reads heavy) | visit |
| physician | doctor |
| utilize | use |
| receive | get |
| assist | help |
| individualized / personalized | made for you |
| minimally invasive | uses small cuts (only if clinically true) |
| non-surgical | without surgery |
| comprehensive | full |
| approximately | about |
| commence | start |
| discomfort | pain |
| prior to | before |
| in order to | to |
| a variety of | many |
| consultation (on buttons) | conversation, visit |

**Check it.** Run `scripts/readability.py` on `content.json` before handing off; QA runs it again on the finished `index.html` (6.1). Fix every block marked `FIX`, then run again. Placeholders like `[City]` count as one short word.

### 4.1 Content files

page-content writes two files in `<work>/content/`:

- `plan.md`: the ordered block list, one block per line starting with its number and full block ID including the variant (for example `2. M01-B: Message 1 · Seen this week`), with the message it carries; benchmark ideas used go on lines after the list. `scripts/wireframe.py` reads this list.
- `content.json`: every word on the page, in this shape:

```json
{
  "meta": { "page_title": "", "meta_description": "", "cta_primary": "", "cta_secondary": "", "keyword": "" },
  "blocks": [
    { "id": "M01-B", "message": "1", "slots": { "h1": "", "who": "", "bullets": [""], "cta": "", "image_brief": "Photo: doctor with patient, 4:5" } }
  ],
  "legal": { "disclaimer": "", "before_after_disclosure": "", "form_note": "Please don't include medical details." },
  "placeholders": ["[City]"],
  "compliance_notes": ["what you could not confirm and why"]
}
```

Slot names follow the block's content slots in 3.1 (headline, bullets, steps, faq items, and so on). Image needs go in `image_brief` slots so the designer knows what each photo should show. Run `scripts/readability.py` on `content.json` and fix every block marked `FIX`.

### 4.2 Lorem pages (placeholder text)

When the brief says `Content: lorem`, the user wants the design and will add the words later. There is no `content.json`; the orchestrator writes `plan.md` and the designer fills the slots in the markup:

- **Lorem ipsum** in headlines, leads, paragraphs, bullets, steps, card text, stats context, testimonials, FAQ questions and answers, and captions.
- **Real-size lorem:** keep the lengths of this section so the layout fits real copy later: headlines up to 8 words, sentences 8 to 12 words, bullets 2 to 6 words, and the slot counts from 3.1. Sentence case, ending with periods; no em dashes.
- **Real words where structure or the law needs them:** the practice name and the logo alt text, button and CTA labels from the brief's actions, navigation labels, the footer legal links, form labels, the form note ("Please don't include medical details.") and the privacy link, and image alt text that says what the photo should show.
- **No invented facts as lorem stand-ins:** numbers, ratings, review counts, prices and phone numbers stay `[placeholders]`.

readability.py is not run on lorem pages; QA lists the copy checks (area A, area B wording) under "Not checked".

### 4.3 Content documents

- Change the document's words only to fit a slot (length, count), to reach grade 5.9 or lower, or to follow compliance (claims, disclaimers). Keep the meaning and the client's voice, and list **every** change in the report (block, slot, before → after, why).
- Write only what the document does not have (for example a button label or a missing FAQ answer), in its voice, and say which parts you wrote. Never invent facts: anything unknown stays a `[placeholder]`.
- If the document conflicts with the brief or with compliance, follow compliance, keep the brief's messages and CTA words, and note the conflict.
