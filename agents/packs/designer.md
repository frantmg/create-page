# Rules: materials (section 2)

## 2. Read the materials

page-content reads the facts, compliance documents, current site facts and voice, and the benchmark. page-designer reads the logo, brand guide and the current site's look. Both follow the priority rule at the end.

- **Logo:** the orchestrator copies it into `<work>/materials/` (from the attachment or the current website) and the brief gives its path, or says `Logo: none`. page-designer always runs `scripts/logo.py` on it: it trims empty space, resizes raster logos to 400px wide at most, writes a data URI (or a cleaned inline SVG), and reports whether the logo is light or dark plus its main color. Embed the result in the header and the footer with `alt="[Practice name] logo"`. A light logo sits only on a dark or brand-color background, never on white. With no logo, use the practice name as a text wordmark in the display font. The logo spot is never empty.
- **Brand guidelines:** pull the primary brand color (the color seed), any secondary or accent color, the fonts, and voice rules. Brand rules win over the defaults in this kit when they conflict, except the reading level and compliance rules.
- **Brand guide = the limits of the design.** When a brand guide is shared, the orchestrator writes `<work>/brand.json` from it before the build, using only what the guide says (never filling a field from taste; write `unspecified` when the guide is silent):

```json
{
  "source": "materials/<brand guide file>",
  "colors": { "primary": "#HEX", "secondary": ["#HEX"], "accent": ["#HEX"], "neutrals": ["#HEX"] },
  "fonts": { "display": "Font name", "body": "Font name" },
  "corners": { "buttons": "square | rounded | pill | unspecified", "media": "square | rounded | unspecified", "radius_px": null },
  "gradients": "allowed | not allowed | unspecified",
  "decorative_elements": "allowed | approval required | not allowed | unspecified",
  "photo_style": "what the guide says about imagery",
  "dont": ["each rule the guide states as a don't, in its own words"],
  "approved": {}
}
```

  With `brand.json` present the page stays inside the guide: its colors (no invented accent color), its fonts as designed, its corner style, and **no decorative style the guide does not allow or mention** (no ornaments, grain, patterns, gradients or uneven shapes). This wins over the art-direction defaults in section 5 (the gradient moment, grain, signature shapes). Where the guide is silent, use the most neutral option (even, plain corners; solid brand color; no decoration) and list each one as an assumption for the user to confirm. Something beyond the guide goes in only when the user explicitly approves it; record it under `approved`. `scripts/qa.py --brand <work>/brand.json` checks the finished page against it.
- **Compliance documents:** list every rule that affects copy, claims, testimonials, images, forms or disclaimers. Follow them strictly. They override everything in this kit, including the reading level if a required disclaimer must be word-for-word.
- **Practice facts:** collect what is real. Anything not provided stays a `[placeholder]`.
- **Content document:** when the user shares their own content (a Word, PDF or text file with the page's words), it is in `<work>/materials/` and the brief says `Content: document`. page-content uses its words as described in `rules/copy.md` 4.3.
- **Current website:** the orchestrator runs `scripts/fetch_site.py` during the intake, which saves `<work>/materials/site/summary.json` (title, phones, emails, address lines, navigation, headings, the colors and fonts the site uses, the logo) and `text.md` (each page's readable text). Read those first; open the site yourself (WebFetch, or the browser if it needs rendering) only for what they do not have. Pull real practice facts (services, providers, locations, hours, insurance, phone), the logo, colors, fonts and voice in use. Treat these facts as provided by the practice, but flag anything that looks out of date. When there is no brand guide, the current site is the brand reference.
- **Benchmark website:** open it and take **structure ideas only**: section order, which kinds of modules it uses, how it leads to the CTA, how the form or quiz flows, how much content it shows. Write 3 to 5 short takeaways and map each one to blocks from the library (3.1). Never take its colors, fonts, imagery, logo or copy.
- **Priority when sources disagree:** compliance documents, then brand guidelines, then the current website, then the art direction in section 5. The benchmark only informs structure and never overrides any of these.

# Rules: design and build (section 5)

## 5. Design and build (art direction, single-file HTML)

Owned by page-designer. Design every page as if the `frontend-design` skill is active (load it with the Skill tool when it is listed; the rules below summarize it). The goal is a page that looks made for this one practice, never a template. Material Design stays the logic underneath (color roles, contrast, state layers, accessible components), but the look is editorial and specific to the brand.

### 5.1 Ground it in the practice

Before designing, name in one line each: the practice's world (specialty, place, patients), the audience, and the page's one job. Distinct choices come from the subject: an orthopedic practice in a mountain town (movement, bone, the outdoors, athletes) should look nothing like a med spa in Scottsdale (skin, light, ritual) or a behavioral health clinic (calm, safety, plain talk). Use the brand guide, logo, photos and real practice facts throughout. Design decisions (color, type, imagery, shape) come from the brand guidelines first, then the current website; a benchmark site never drives the look.

### 5.2 Write a design plan, then review it

Before any code, write a short plan:

- **Color:** 4 to 6 named hex values (for example "Summit navy #0B2545", "Trail blue #1463FF", "Snowfield #F2F6FB"), built from the brand color and its tonal palette (`scripts/m3_scheme.py`). Say where each one is used.
- **Type:** the typefaces (brand fonts first) and their roles, plus the one type scale for this project, picked from the ranges in 5.5.
- **Layout:** one sentence and a small ASCII wireframe for the hero and for the overall page rhythm. Say how content aligns, and which benchmark structure ideas you used, if any. State every planned overlap (which element crosses into which section, and the space that section reserves for it).
- **Principles:** 2 or 3 lines on what makes this page unique, and the **one** memorable element where the boldness is spent (for example: a full-bleed brand gradient field behind the hero, a hero photo that breaks the grid, a distinctive display face set with a strong headline shape).

Then review the plan against the brief. If any part reads like the default you would make for any similar practice, change it and say what you changed and why. Check it against the cliché list in 5.3. Show the plan to the user only when they asked to approve the plan first; otherwise keep it in your working notes and build.

### 5.3 Clichés to avoid (unless the brand guide asks for them)

- Purple or violet gradients, purple-to-pink, and any gradient that is not built from the brand's own colors.
- Inter, Roboto, Open Sans, Lato, Montserrat, Poppins, Space Grotesk, Playfair Display, or system defaults as the main type.
- The SaaS card kit: content chopped into identical rounded cards, one radius on everything, the same soft grey shadow under each.
- A warm cream background (near #F4F1EA) with a serif display and a terracotta or clay accent (near #D97757).
- A near-black background with one acid-green or vermilion accent.
- Broadsheet layouts with hairline rules and zero radius everywhere.
- Template chrome: tracked all-caps eyebrows above every heading, "A · B · C" meta strings, "→" on every link, tinted near-black (#0B0B0B, #111) standing in for black, monospace for small labels.
- Big number + small label + gradient accent as the default hero.
- Numbered markers (01, 02, 03) on content that is not a sequence. Steps and timelines may be numbered; reasons and benefits may not.
- Fade-and-slide-up on every section, hover effects on every card.

### 5.4 Color and gradients

- The brand color is the seed. Run `scripts/m3_scheme.py "#HEX"` to get Material color roles (for accessible text and surface pairs) and the full tonal palettes (tones 0 to 100) of the brand, secondary, tertiary and neutral hues.
- Use color with intent, not timidly: at least one full-bleed section in a strong brand tone, and one gradient moment built only from brand tones (for example primary tone 20 to primary tone 50, or primary to tertiary), with optional fine grain (an inline SVG noise at low opacity) so it feels printed, not digital. Keep gradients to one or two big moments (hero field, final CTA band), never as decoration on every element.
- Neutrals come from the brand's neutral palette (slightly tinted toward the brand hue), not pure greys. True black is #000 only if the brand uses it.
- Every text and background pair meets WCAG 2.2 AA. Use the role pairs from `scripts/m3_scheme.py` (`on-primary` on `primary`, and so on) or check contrast.

### 5.5 Typography

**Fonts.** Use the approved brand fonts and verify that they load (`scripts/qa.py` lists the loaded families). If a brand font is unavailable, say so and propose a substitute; never swap an approved font silently. Keep the family count to one or two. When the brand names no fonts, pick one distinct pairing (Google Fonts) that fits the practice, and do not reuse the same pairing on every project:

| Feel | Display | Body | Good for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Warm, human | Fraunces (use its optical size and soft axis) | Hanken Grotesk | Family practice, women's health, pediatrics |
| Refined, quiet luxury | Instrument Serif | Instrument Sans | Aesthetics, plastics, med spa, dermatology |
| Energetic, athletic | Bricolage Grotesque (play with width) | Source Serif 4 | Orthopedics, sports medicine, PT, chiropractic |
| Trusted, institutional | Newsreader (large optical size) | Public Sans | Multi-specialty groups, surgery centers |
| Friendly, bright | Young Serif | Figtree | Dental, pediatrics, primary care |
| Calm, very readable | Literata | Atkinson Hyperlegible | Behavioral health, older audiences, eye care |

**Scale.** These are flexible starting ranges. Choose **one coherent scale per project** based on the font's proportions, the brand, content length and viewport, then reuse that small set of styles everywhere. Do not pick a different size for every module.

| Role | Desktop | Mobile | Line height |
|---|---|---|---|
| H1 / hero heading | 48 to 64px | 34 to 42px | 1.05 to 1.18 |
| H2 / section heading | 36 to 44px | 28 to 34px | 1.15 to 1.25 |
| H3 / module heading | 22 to 28px | 21 to 24px | 1.2 to 1.35 |
| H4 / supporting heading | 18 to 20px | 18 to 20px | 1.3 to 1.4 |
| Intro / lead | 19 to 21px | 18 to 19px | 1.5 to 1.65 |
| Body | 17 to 18px | 16 to 18px | 1.5 to 1.7 |
| Eyebrow | 12 to 14px | 12 to 13px | 1.3 to 1.5 |
| Caption / supporting note | 13 to 14px | 13 to 14px | 1.4 to 1.6 |
| Button label | 15 to 17px | 15 to 17px | 1.2 to 1.4 |

Put the chosen scale in the page's token block as CSS custom properties (for example `--text-h1`, `--text-h2`), with `clamp()` between the mobile and desktop values.

**Rules**
- Keep a visible difference between heading levels. Use semantic heading levels by content structure, independent of visual size.
- Use regular or medium weights when the font gives enough hierarchy. Reserve bold for emphasis; do not make every heading heavy by default.
- Body copy around 45 to 75 characters per line on desktop. Let lines get narrower on mobile; never shrink text to keep desktop wrapping.
- Aim for intentional headline shapes. Adjust text width and scale before adding manual line breaks. Avoid a lone last word and awkward short lines (`text-wrap: balance` on headings, `text-wrap: pretty` on paragraphs).
- Tighten heading tracking slightly only when the font benefits. No cramped letters, clipped accents, or extra tracking in body copy.
- Uppercase and wider tracking only for short labels, used selectively, following the brand's capitalization conventions.
- Eyebrows are optional. Omit them when they repeat the heading.
- Do not rely on pale text, very thin weights or tiny type to look sophisticated.
- Sentence case for headings and buttons unless the brand says otherwise. Form input text at least 16px on mobile.

### 5.6 Layout, spacing and motion

- **Strong asymmetry:** a 12-column grid with uneven spans (7/5, 8/4, 5/7), offset starts, media that breaks the container edge, and overlaps (a photo under the headline, a form card overlapping two sections). Avoid centered, symmetric stacks except for one deliberate moment.
- **Zero fluff padding:** spacing comes from a scale tied to the body line-height (for example 0.5x to 6x). Each section gets the space its content needs, not one blanket section padding. No empty wrapper divs, no decorative spacers. Tight is fine; cramped is not.
- **Hierarchy over boxes:** separate content with scale, color fields and alignment before reaching for borders, cards or shadows. When cards are right (directories, provider grids), keep them flat and vary emphasis (one featured item larger) where the content allows.
- **Shadows:** follow the brand guide when it specifies shadows. Otherwise never use short, dark, tight shadows (like `0 1px 3px rgba(0,0,0,.3)` or stock component-library elevations). Use shadows that are **wider and more transparent**: a large blur, a soft downward offset, a negative spread so the edge fades, and low opacity (about 0.06 to 0.14), tinted with the darkest brand neutral instead of pure black. Starting points:
  - Soft (resting cards, panels): `0 12px 32px -8px rgb(<ink> / 0.08)`
  - Raised (forms, overlapping panels): `0 18px 48px -12px rgb(<ink> / 0.10)`
  - Floating (sticky bars, menus, dialogs): `0 24px 64px -16px rgb(<ink> / 0.12)`
  Use them only where a surface truly floats or overlaps; flat content stays flat. Set them once as `--shadow-soft`, `--shadow-raised` and `--shadow-float` in the token block (`templates/page.html`) so every block shares them.
- **Alignment:** keep the asymmetry, but make every position deliberate.
  - Every layout sits on a 12-column grid with one max width and gutter, set as tokens (`--grid-max`, `--grid-gutter`, `--col`; see `templates/page.html`). Place things with `grid-column` spans, not one-off margins.
  - Offsets and bleeds are welcome, but each one snaps to a named edge: a grid column line, the H1's top or baseline, or a section edge. An element never starts or ends at an arbitrary position.
  - When an element overlaps into the next section, that section reserves the space (padding or a matching column offset) so its content never sits under or beside the overlap by accident. State the overlap in the design plan (5.2).
  - Rows inside a column (proof row, dividers, button groups) match their column's width; stat items use equal columns.
  - Hero media is vertically anchored: either its top aligns with the eyebrow or H1 top, or it fills the hero height and bleeds by a set token amount (`--bleed`).
- **Mobile:** design the 390px view first. Keep asymmetry through bleed, offsets and type scale, not tiny columns.
- **Motion:** two moments at most that the user did not trigger: one orchestrated hero entrance (headline, media and CTA in one short sequence) and the CTA attention motion in conversion mode. Motion that answers an action (accordion opening, form step changing, video opening) is welcome. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.

### 5.7 Block treatments in HTML and CSS

Semantic HTML and the page's inline CSS do layout, spacing, type and color. Interactive parts use native elements first and small vanilla JS only when needed, restyled with the tokens so nothing looks like a stock widget.

**Wireframe parity: every block must look better than its wireframe, never worse.** The wireframe (`blocks/<ID>-*.html`, what the user approved in the block review) sets the block's structure, the arrangement of its parts and **where the interaction happens** (a handle on the image, hotspots on a figure, tabs beside their panel). Keep all of that; the art direction goes on top. Never swap an interaction for a generic browser control somewhere else (for example a slider bar under a before/after photo). Before handing off, compare each built block with its wireframe side by side.

**Blocks define structure and content slots only.** Never copy a block's `<style>`, class styles or inline styles into the page. Restyle every block from the page's token block. In particular, no `text-transform: uppercase` on buttons, headings or nav; uppercase is allowed only on eyebrows and short labels, and only if the brand uses it.

| Block | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Heroes (M01, M02) | Headline from the H1 range with a deliberate shape on the grid, media bleeding off one edge, brand color field or gradient behind. CTA is a `.btn` link with the unique CTA shape |
| Feature cards (M08) | Not three equal cards. An editorial list: large titles with short text on an asymmetric grid, or one featured item with two smaller ones; items in a row share one top line, never offset vertically |
| Stats (M10) | One stat larger in the display face (within the H1 to H2 range), the others smaller beside it, each with plain context; all stats share one top line in equal columns, never offset vertically |
| Steps (M11) | Real sequence, so numbers are allowed: display numerals (H2 size) in an `<ol>`; all steps share one top line and equal columns on desktop, stacked on mobile. Show the sequence with a thin connecting line or arrow between numerals, not by offsetting steps vertically. The section CTA sits centered under the steps or aligned to the grid's first column with the steps spanning the full width |
| Pull quote and reviews (M12, M26) | One large quote set in the display face in a `<blockquote>`; other reviews smaller in a scroll-snap row. Stars in a brand tone |
| Tabs (M13) | `role="tablist"` with buttons and the tabs script from `templates/page.html` (arrow keys, Home, End); vertical on desktop, scrollable on mobile, restyled indicator |
| Comparison (M14) | Plain `<table>` with a caption and the recommended column on a brand color field |
| FAQ (M31) | Native `<details>` and `<summary>` (keyboard accessible with no JS), no shadow, large question type, rules between items |
| Forms (M29, M30) | Native `<input>`, `<select>` and `<label>` styled with the tokens (custom radius, focus color, label style). Multi-step form: one `<fieldset>` per step with a progress bar and a few lines of JS. Quiz: large tappable option cards built on radio inputs |
| Video (M22) | Poster image with a play `<button>`; a native `<dialog>` opens the player, which loads only on click |
| Photo mosaic (M23-A) | An asymmetric grid on the column lines: one large photo with smaller ones beside it, short labels on the photos, the "+ N photos" tile as a link; not a carousel |
| Before/after (M23-B) | The comparison is **on the photo**: a full-height divider with a round handle centred on it, the before photo on the left and the after photo revealed on the right, "Before" and "After" labels pinned in the top corners (never inside the moving layer, so they are not clipped). Drag anywhere on the photo; keyboard and screen readers work through the invisible native range input covering it. Use the tested `.compare` component in `templates/page.html` and restyle it with the tokens. Text column beside it on desktop (condition and treatment, the "Results vary" consent line), below it on mobile. Never a separate slider bar under the photo |
| Body map (M17) | The figure stays: each area is a real `<button>` placed on the illustration (percent positions on the grid of the figure), `aria-pressed` on the chosen one, and the condition list beside it updates to that area; the same buttons work on mobile with the list below. Never replace the figure with a plain list |
| Finder bar (M33) | One row on desktop (stacked on mobile) of native selects styled with the tokens and the main button, set as a single bar on its own field so it reads as one tool, as in the wireframe |
| Carousel (M24) | CSS scroll-snap with previous and next buttons; no carousel library |
| Header and mobile bar (G1, G2, LP) | Thin custom header; the mobile menu script from `templates/page.html` (`aria-expanded`, Escape closes); sticky call/book bar on mobile |
| Insurance strip (M25) | Logos in one tonal color, set on a quiet band, not boxed |
| Icons | Inline SVG with `aria-hidden="true"` (or a label when it carries meaning), used sparingly |

### 5.8 Stack and build

Every page is **one hand-written HTML file**: semantic HTML plus one inline `<style>` and, only where a block needs it, a small inline `<script>`. It is built from the blocks in `blocks/` (structure and content slots) and starts from `templates/page.html`. There are no build tools, installs or frameworks: the file you write is the file that is delivered.

Structure:

```
<work>/
  brief.md
  content/plan.md, content/content.json   # from page-content; the designer never edits them
  index.html                              # the page: tokens, block styles, markup, small JS
  alternates.html, tweak-options.json     # tweak support (5.10); never linked from index.html
  tweak.html                              # made by scripts/tweakbar.py for the tweak round; never delivered
```

Rules:
- **Start from `templates/page.html`.** Copy it to `<work>/index.html`, set the kit version in the `<!-- Built with create-page vX.Y.Z -->` line, replace the fonts with the design plan's (2 families max, `display=swap`) and paste the color roles from `scripts/m3_scheme.py`.
- **One token source:** colors (Material roles plus the plan's named colors), type scale, spacing, grid (`--grid-max`, `--grid-gutter`, `--col`), radius and shadows live once as CSS custom properties in the token block at the top of the `<style>`. Block styles use only `var(--...)`; no raw hex, sizes or shadows repeated in blocks.
- **One section per block:** `<section data-block="M01-B">` (or `header`/`footer` for G blocks) in the order of `plan.md`, with that block's styles grouped and commented by block ID. Block sections are direct children of `<main>` (no wrappers), so the tweak bar can move, hide and swap them. Wrap each block's CSS group in markers, `/* block:M01-B */` before it and `/* /block:M01-B */` after it, so `scripts/apply_tweaks.py` can move or remove a block's CSS with its markup.
- **Every word comes from `content.json`:** copy each slot into the markup exactly as written, including alt text and labels. Never type, cut or reword copy.
- **Production-grade HTML and CSS:** semantic elements and landmarks, mobile-first media queries, short readable class names, no dead CSS, no inline `style` attributes, no `!important` outside the reduced-motion rule.
- **JavaScript:** none by default. Use small inline vanilla JS only where a block needs it (mobile menu, tabs, multi-step form, video dialog), always keyboard accessible, and delete the template's scripts the page does not use. No frameworks or libraries.
- Forms do not submit anywhere real. Mark the action with `<!-- HIPAA-compliant form endpoint required -->`.
- **Speed, without giving up design:** at most 2 font families (`display=swap`), images as compressed WebP with `loading="lazy"` below the fold (the logo inline as SVG or a data URI), video loads only on click. Aim for about **60 KB for `index.html` before images**. Gradients, grain, SVG shapes and color fields are nearly free in CSS, so speed is never a reason to drop them.
- The page is `<work>/index.html`: open it directly in a browser. Run the checks in section 6 on it.

### 5.9 Quality floor (always, without announcing it)

Responsive from 360px up, visible keyboard focus, `prefers-reduced-motion` respected, WCAG 2.2 AA contrast, 48px touch targets, one `<h1>`, ordered headings, labels on every field, alt text on every image, skip link. Images: the user's photos when given; otherwise placeholder boxes with the right aspect ratio and a label saying what photo goes there. Never use stock photos of real-looking patients presented as actual patients. No third-party tracking scripts.

### 5.10 Tweak support and applying tweaks

The user finishes the design in the tweak round (START-HERE.md 0.7) with `scripts/tweakbar.py`, which wraps `index.html` in a live preview with a tweak bar. The bar reads the token block, so everything in 5.8's "one token source" rule is what makes it work. The designer adds two files next to `index.html` with the build:

**`<work>/alternates.html`: other versions of the planned blocks.** For each planned block that has library variants (M01, M02, M03, M05, M08, M11, M22, M23, M26, M28: A, B and C), build the other variants, at most 2 per block, held to the same standard as the page: the page's tokens, art direction, mobile and ADA rules, and the words from `content.json` exactly. Leave out a slot the content has no words for rather than writing new copy; page-content fills it if the user picks that version. Format:

```html
<style data-variant="M05-A">
/* M05-A: image below text */
.m05a { ... only var(--...) values, scoped by the version's own class ... }
</style>
<template data-alt-for="M05-B" data-variant="M05-A" data-label="Image below text">
  <section data-block="M05-A" class="m05a">...</section>
</template>
```

`data-alt-for` is the planned block's ID as it appears in `index.html`; the template holds exactly one `<section data-block>`. Each version's CSS goes in its own `<style data-variant="ID">` (apply_tweaks.py moves it into the page when the user picks that version) and is scoped to the version's own class so it cannot change the page. This file does not count toward the page size budget.

**`<work>/tweak-options.json`: palettes and font pairings to try.**

```json
{
  "palettes": [{ "name": "Deeper teal", "tokens": { "--md-sys-color-primary": "#..." } }],
  "fonts": [{ "name": "Fraunces + Figtree", "display": "\"Fraunces\", Georgia, serif", "body": "\"Figtree\", system-ui, sans-serif",
              "href": "https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Fraunces:opsz,wght@9..144,400..700&family=Figtree:wght@400;600&display=swap" }],
  "brand_locked": { "colors": false, "fonts": false }
}
```

- **Palettes (2 or 3):** each holds the full `:root` colour roles from `scripts/m3_scheme.py`, from another variant of the brand colour (`--variant tonal_spot`, `vibrant`, `content`) or from a second brand or logo colour. When the brief or brand guide fixes the colours, offer only variants of the brand colour and set `brand_locked.colors` to true.
- **Font pairings (2 or 3):** Google Fonts with `display=swap`, each following 5.5 and avoiding the 5.3 clichés. When the brief names brand fonts, set `brand_locked.fonts` to true and offer pairings only if the brand guide allows a second face; otherwise leave `fonts` empty.

**Applying a `tweaks.json`:** `scripts/apply_tweaks.py` does the mechanical part in seconds: the Shape tab's real-value rules (corners, shadows, page width, section spacing, measured from the page as built and written as a `/* block:shape-tweaks */` group, phone and desktop spacing in their own media queries), tokens and palettes (a seed palette recomputed with `m3_scheme.py`), scales written as real token values, fonts, page width, button shape, block order, hidden blocks with their CSS, block versions (markup and `<style data-variant>` CSS, the old block kept in `alternates.html`), backgrounds (`band-plain`, `band-tint-low`, `band-tint`, `band-brand`, `band-dark` on the token pairs) and the `final` text edits from page-content. The designer gets only its `needs_designer` and `cleanup` lists:

- `blocks.replacements` and `blocks.added`: build each new block from `blocks/` in the page's style, at the place `plan.md` gives (a replacement takes the old block's section, and its CSS is removed), with the words from `content.json`, to the same standard as the first build and with block CSS markers.
- Contrast failures: keep the user's choice as close as possible and adjust the failing role's tone until it passes; say what changed. Check the blocks whose background changed for AA contrast inside (buttons, links, captions).
- Notes: do what they ask when it is design; send anything about words to page-content as a copy change request.
- Cleanup: CSS left behind where a block had no markers; remove it (or move it into `alternates.html` as that version's `<style data-variant>`).
- Pinned comments (`comments`): a note on one element; do what it asks within these rules, or send it back as a copy change request when it is about words.
- Photos attached in the chat (listed when they were too big to send from the tweak page): put each in its element as a compressed WebP (about 1600px wide at most, `loading="lazy"` below the fold) with the user's alt text.

**Element changes and photos** (from the tweak bar's "Select element") are applied by the script: each changed element gets its own class, and its rule in the `/* block:element-tweaks */` group uses only the page's tokens (`var(--md-sys-color-...)`, `var(--text-...)`, `var(--space-...)`, `var(--radius-...)`), with a tripled class so it outranks the block's own rules without `!important`. Photos are embedded as compressed data URIs with the user's alt text. When the designer later edits such an element, keep its class and rule, or fold the rule into the block's own CSS.

Then refresh `alternates.html` and `tweak-options.json` for any block or setting that changed, so the next tweak page has choices again, and rerun 6.1. Never copy tweak bar code or `data-tw-` attributes into `index.html` (qa.py `tweak_leftovers`).

### 5.11 Brand guide limits

When `<work>/brand.json` exists (`rules/materials.md`), the brand guide sets the limits and wins over this section's art-direction defaults: use its colors (no accent color it does not have; tones of its own colors from `m3_scheme.py` are fine), its fonts as designed (no widened or narrowed widths it does not use), its corner style, and leave out every decorative style it does not allow or mention: ornaments, grain, patterns, gradients (use solid brand color instead) and uneven or signature shapes. The memorable element then comes from layout, scale, photography and color blocking within the guide. Where the guide is silent, use the most neutral option and list it under "Brand assumptions" in the design report. Anything beyond the guide only with the user's explicit approval, recorded under `approved` in brand.json. `qa.py --brand` checks fonts, corners, gradients, decorations and colors against it.

# Rules: QA and review (section 6)

## 6. QA and review

page-qa runs all of this; page-designer runs 6.1 on its own build and page-content runs `readability.py` on `content.json` before handing off. Never report a check as done unless it was actually run.

**qa.py runs once per stage.** While building or fixing, the designer checks with `--fast` (390 and 1440px only). The last thing the designer does in a stage is one full run (all five widths) saved with `--out <work>/qa/auto.json`. The reviewers use that file as the automated results when its `run` is `full` and its `page_hash` matches the page; they run qa.py themselves only when the file is missing, was a fast run, or the page has changed since.

**Three reviewers in parallel.** The review is split across three fresh page-qa agents started at the same time: reviewer 1 takes areas A, B and F, reviewer 2 area C, reviewer 3 areas D and E. Each returns the 6.3 report for its areas; the orchestrator merges them into one report (the verdict is FAIL if any reviewer found a blocker or major).

### 6.1 Automated checks

axe-core ships with the kit at `scripts/vendor/axe.min.js`, so there is nothing to install for it. Playwright is usually installed; if not: `pip install --break-system-packages playwright && python3 -m playwright install chromium`.

1. Run `python3 <kit>/scripts/qa.py <work>/index.html --axe <kit>/scripts/vendor/axe.min.js --brand-fonts "<brand fonts from the brief>" --out <work>/qa/auto.json` (leave `--brand-fonts` out when the brief names none; add `--brand <work>/brand.json` when it exists; add `--fast` only for self-checks while working). It checks 320, 390, 768, 1024 and 1440px for overflow, small tap targets and small input text; runs axe-core for WCAG 2.2 A/AA; tabs through the page for focus order and visible focus; checks the logo in the header and the footer at 390 and 1440px (an `img`, `svg` or `.wordmark` that is visible, at least 24px tall and has 3:1 contrast against its background); reports loaded fonts, the phone first screen (H1 and actions), CTA labels, em dashes, block IDs and the file size; checks alignment at 390 and 1440px (edge near-misses 1 to 12px apart, content overlapping content in another section, rows wider than their text column, and at 1440px items in one row whose tops differ by more than 2px) and style (uppercase on buttons, `a.btn` and nav links, main fonts from the 5.3 cliché list unless passed with `--brand-fonts`); reports `tweak_leftovers` (tweak bar code that must never be in the page); with `--brand` checks fonts, button and media corners, gradients, decorative images and off-brand colors against the brand guide (`brand.problems`); saves screenshots at 390 and 1440px (plus the 390 x 844 phone first screen).
2. Run `python3 <kit>/scripts/readability.py <work>/index.html`. The copy is in the HTML itself, so it grades the page directly.

### 6.2 Review areas

On a lorem page (`Content: lorem`, `rules/copy.md` 4.2), skip area A and the area B wording checks and list them under "Not checked"; still check that the real words 4.2 requires are present (CTA labels, legal links, form labels and note, alt text) and that no stats, ratings or reviews were invented.

Look at every screenshot (desktop, 390px, phone first screen) and read the source. Then check each area:

**A. Copy and brief**
- Reading grade 5.9 or lower for every block and the page; no sentence over 15 words; medical terms explained.
- H1 says Message 1 (in conversion mode: the offer in the keyword's words, with Message 1 right under it). Messages 2 and 3 appear next, in order.
- Primary CTA in the hero, mid-page and at the end, with identical words; at most 2 distinct actions.
- No em dashes; none of the generated-page tells from section 4; buttons verb-first, sentence case.

**B. Compliance** (the client's documents win)
- No guaranteed outcomes ("cure", "100%", "pain-free for life").
- No invented facts: stats, reviews, ratings, awards, credentials, insurance names, hours. Every unknown is a visible `[placeholder]`.
- Testimonials and before/after only if real, approved and consented, with "Results vary." nearby; FTC note if reviews were incentivized.
- Forms ask only what is needed; no free-text symptom fields unless allowed; "Please don't include medical details" and privacy link; unchecked TCPA consent box if SMS is used; submit target marked `<!-- HIPAA-compliant form endpoint required -->`.
- No third-party tracking scripts; flag pixels on form or condition pages.
- Footer legal links: Privacy policy, Notice of privacy practices (HIPAA), Accessibility, Non-discrimination notice, Terms. Medical disclaimer in conversion mode.
- Provider names and credentials exactly as given; "Medically reviewed by" only with a real reviewer.

**C. Design quality**
- Brand fidelity: colors, fonts and voice match the brand guide (or the current website); the benchmark did not drive the look.
- Logo: the real logo from the brief (or a text wordmark only when the brief says `Logo: none`) in the header and the footer, visible, at least 24px tall, 3:1 contrast against its background (qa.py `logo`), with `alt="[Practice name] logo"`; a light logo is never on white. A missing or invisible logo is a **major** finding.
- Typography (5.5): brand fonts actually loaded, one scale used throughout, clear heading levels, balanced headline wraps with no lone last word, body lines around 45 to 75 characters, no pale, thin or tiny text, uppercase only on short labels.
- Art direction (5.2 to 5.6): one clear memorable element; brand color fields and the gradient moment are present; nothing from the cliché list in 5.3; asymmetry with clear alignment; no fluff padding or doubled section spacing; shadows wide and transparent (or per brand); no stock component look (default all-caps buttons, default browser controls, tight dark elevations).
- Alignment: every offset snaps to a grid line or named edge; planned overlaps only; rows match their column. qa.py `alignment` lists near-misses, overlaps and wide rows.
- Row tops: sibling items in one row (steps, cards, stats, list items) share a top edge within 2px on desktop; steps show sequence with a connecting line or arrow, never a vertical stagger (qa.py `alignment.row_tops_w1440`).
- Wireframe parity: each block keeps its wireframe's structure, arrangement and where the interaction happens (`blocks/<ID>-*.html`), and looks better, not worse. An interaction moved into a generic control (for example a slider bar under a before/after photo) or a block plainer than its wireframe is a **major** finding.
- No uppercase on buttons, `a.btn` or nav links (qa.py `style.uppercase_buttons_and_nav`), and no block styles copied from `blocks/`. Cliché main fonts are flagged by qa.py `style`; a cliché font is fine only when the brief names it as the brand font.
- Restraint: if an icon, border, shadow, label, shape or container can be removed without losing meaning, it is a finding.
- A conversion page is never plainer than a regular page would be.

**D. Mobile and responsive**
- No horizontal overflow at any width; no clipped controls, crowded columns or overlaps that hide text.
- Phone first screen (390 x 844) shows the H1, the who line and the primary CTA in conversion mode.
- Layout recomposed for mobile (not just shrunk); sensible stacking order; important images keep useful size.
- `index.html` about 60 KB or less, before images and video (qa.py reports the file size).

**E. ADA (WCAG 2.2 AA)**
- axe-core: zero critical or serious violations; list moderate ones.
- Keyboard: every link, button and field reachable by Tab in a logical order, focus always visible, skip link works, no keyboard traps (accordion, tabs, dialog, mobile menu, quiz).
- Contrast AA for text, buttons, captions and text on photos (check the real screenshots, not just tokens).
- Reflow at 320px with no loss of content; text resizes to 200% without breaking.
- Every image has meaningful alt text (decorative images `alt=""`); video has a transcript link.
- One H1, headings in order, landmarks (`header`, `nav`, `main`, `footer`), page `lang` and `title`.
- Form fields have visible labels; errors say what is wrong and how to fix it; required fields are marked in text, not color alone.
- Tap targets at least 24px (WCAG 2.2 minimum) and 44 to 48px for main actions; `prefers-reduced-motion` respected; no meaning carried by color alone; link text makes sense out of context.

**F. Conversion mode only** (section 3.4)
- Recipe order respected; FAQ below the conversion zone; keyword block below the fold; form type matches the lead flow; final CTA matches the hero.

### 6.3 QA report format (page-qa returns exactly this)

```
QA REPORT  (create-page vX.Y.Z)
Verdict: PASS | FAIL        Round: <n>
Summary: <2 sentences>

Findings
| ID | Severity | Type | Area | Block | Problem | Evidence | Fix |
|----|----------|------|------|-------|---------|----------|-----|
| Q1 | blocker  | judgment | B | M26-B | ...   | ...      | ... |

Areas reviewed: <A, B, F | C | D, E | all>
Automated results: <reused <work>/qa/auto.json (page_hash ...) / ran qa.py myself, and why>
Passed: <areas or checks that passed>
Not checked: <anything that could not run, and why>
```

**Type**
- **mechanical:** a fix the designer can make and verify with qa.py, with no design judgment: contrast values (a token's tone), missing alt text, uppercase on labels, buttons or nav, tap target size. Fixed without a re-review, whatever the severity, and verified by the designer's full qa.py run.
- **judgment:** everything else.

**Severity**
- **Blocker:** compliance or legal risk (invented facts, guarantees, missing required disclaimer, PHI risk), axe critical or serious, keyboard trap, page broken or overflowing at any width, H1 or CTA missing from the phone first screen in conversion mode, reading grade over 5.9.
- **Major:** anything outside the brand guide (qa.py `brand.problems` not empty, or a style the guide does not allow), tweak bar code in the page (qa.py `tweak_leftovers` not empty), brief not followed (messages, CTA words, action count), alignment near-misses or unplanned overlaps (qa.py `alignment`), row items whose tops differ by more than 2px on desktop, uppercase on a button, `a.btn` or nav link, logo missing, invisible, under 24px or under 3:1 contrast in the header or footer, brand font missing without a flag, a cliché from 5.3, contrast or focus issues outside axe, clearly plain or template-like design, mobile layout poorly recomposed.
- **Minor:** polish (wraps, spacing, small inconsistencies, optional refinements).

**Verdict:** FAIL if there is any blocker or major finding; otherwise PASS (minors listed for the record).

**Re-review:** after a fix round, a fresh page-qa re-checks the blocker and judgment-major findings (the reviewers of the affected areas, or one reviewer for all), plus the automated results. Mechanical and minor findings are not re-reviewed; the designer's full qa.py run verifies them.

The Area column lets the orchestrator route each finding to its owner (0.6): A and B wording to page-content, C, D, E and B build issues to page-designer.

# Rules: copy at grade 5 (section 4)

## 4. Write the copy at grade 5

Owned by page-content. Every word goes in `content/content.json` (shape in 4.1); the designer renders it as written. The intake sets the content mode: **write** (page-content writes every word), **document** (the client's own content document is the source, 4.3) or **lorem** (placeholder text, 4.2). Every word on the page is written for a 5th-grade reader. Target Flesch-Kincaid grade **5.0**, hard maximum **5.9** for the page and for each block.

**Rules**

- Short sentences. Average 8 to 12 words. Never more than 15.
- Short words. Prefer one and two syllables.
- Talk to the reader: "you" and "we".
- Active voice. One idea per sentence.
- Headlines: 8 words or fewer, say the benefit. ("Walk without knee pain again.")
- Buttons: start with a verb, 2 to 4 words. ("Book a visit", "Call now", "Check my insurance".)
- Bullets: 2 to 6 words each.
- Medical names are allowed when they are the real name of a condition or treatment. Say the name once, then explain it in plain words right after. ("Sciatica is pain that runs from your low back down your leg.") Pass these words to the checker with `--allow`.
- No em dashes. Use periods, commas, colons or parentheses.
- Avoid generated-page tells: no single word in a headline set in a different color or italic, no uppercase except selectively on short labels, no labels above every heading, no meta strings joined with middle dots, no "→" added to button or link text.
- Never trade accuracy for simplicity. If a plain word changes the medical meaning, keep the right word and explain it.

**Swaps**

| Instead of | Write |
|---|---|
| appointment (when it reads heavy) | visit |
| physician | doctor |
| utilize | use |
| receive | get |
| assist | help |
| individualized / personalized | made for you |
| minimally invasive | uses small cuts (only if clinically true) |
| non-surgical | without surgery |
| comprehensive | full |
| approximately | about |
| commence | start |
| discomfort | pain |
| prior to | before |
| in order to | to |
| a variety of | many |
| consultation (on buttons) | conversation, visit |

**Check it.** Run `scripts/readability.py` on `content.json` before handing off; QA runs it again on the finished `index.html` (6.1). Fix every block marked `FIX`, then run again. Placeholders like `[City]` count as one short word.

### 4.1 Content files

page-content writes two files in `<work>/content/`:

- `plan.md`: the ordered block list, one block per line starting with its number and full block ID including the variant (for example `2. M01-B: Message 1 · Seen this week`), with the message it carries; benchmark ideas used go on lines after the list. `scripts/wireframe.py` reads this list.
- `content.json`: every word on the page, in this shape:

```json
{
  "meta": { "page_title": "", "meta_description": "", "cta_primary": "", "cta_secondary": "", "keyword": "" },
  "blocks": [
    { "id": "M01-B", "message": "1", "slots": { "h1": "", "who": "", "bullets": [""], "cta": "", "image_brief": "Photo: doctor with patient, 4:5" } }
  ],
  "legal": { "disclaimer": "", "before_after_disclosure": "", "form_note": "Please don't include medical details." },
  "placeholders": ["[City]"],
  "compliance_notes": ["what you could not confirm and why"]
}
```

Slot names follow the block's content slots in 3.1 (headline, bullets, steps, faq items, and so on). Image needs go in `image_brief` slots so the designer knows what each photo should show. Run `scripts/readability.py` on `content.json` and fix every block marked `FIX`.

### 4.2 Lorem pages (placeholder text)

When the brief says `Content: lorem`, the user wants the design and will add the words later. There is no `content.json`; the orchestrator writes `plan.md` and the designer fills the slots in the markup:

- **Lorem ipsum** in headlines, leads, paragraphs, bullets, steps, card text, stats context, testimonials, FAQ questions and answers, and captions.
- **Real-size lorem:** keep the lengths of this section so the layout fits real copy later: headlines up to 8 words, sentences 8 to 12 words, bullets 2 to 6 words, and the slot counts from 3.1. Sentence case, ending with periods; no em dashes.
- **Real words where structure or the law needs them:** the practice name and the logo alt text, button and CTA labels from the brief's actions, navigation labels, the footer legal links, form labels, the form note ("Please don't include medical details.") and the privacy link, and image alt text that says what the photo should show.
- **No invented facts as lorem stand-ins:** numbers, ratings, review counts, prices and phone numbers stay `[placeholders]`.

readability.py is not run on lorem pages; QA lists the copy checks (area A, area B wording) under "Not checked".

### 4.3 Content documents

When the brief says `Content: document`, the client's content document (in `<work>/materials/`) is the source of the words:

- Use the document's own words and order where they fit the plan; map each part to the block and slot it belongs to.
- Change the document's words only to fit a slot (length, count), to reach grade 5.9 or lower, or to follow compliance (claims, disclaimers). Keep the meaning and the client's voice, and list **every** change in the report (block, slot, before → after, why).
- Write only what the document does not have (for example a button label or a missing FAQ answer), in its voice, and say which parts you wrote. Never invent facts: anything unknown stays a `[placeholder]`.
- If the document conflicts with the brief or with compliance, follow compliance, keep the brief's messages and CTA words, and note the conflict.
